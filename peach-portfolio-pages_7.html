Background

Before any of this, I was part of my family's footwear manufacturing business, where I built a real foundation in how to run a business — quality control, brand and organizational management, and market research into new markets to sell into. We handled production, manufacturing, and distribution all at once, and I managed a team of 25+ people through it. That kind of hands-on micro and SME business experience is the foundation everything else I've done is built on.

When I relocated to Thailand, I moved straight into the hotel industry as a Guest Service Agent, which is where I learned the basics of how a hotel actually runs day to day — front desk operations, handling guest requests and complaints, check-in and check-out flow, and how every department has to connect for things to run smoothly. From there I became a Financial Income Auditor, which is where I actually learned how rates, ADR, and the other core hotel metrics work and why they matter, and where I started getting closer to OTA management. That role also covered condominium leasing, which taught me how long-term rental procedures work. As a Guest Service Supervisor, I managed 70+ staff across multiple departments, handled complaint resolution and guest communication, and acted as the person in charge during my working hours — including real, practical emergency and panic situations that had to be controlled in the moment.

Now

Today I work in Hotel Tech Software Sales as a Sales & Revenue Executive. To be clear about what that actually means: I'm not managing a hotel's revenue for them — I'm selling technology and RevBoost's revenue management service, and the actual work is pulling in new clients, building trust, and figuring out what's broken in their business before they've fully seen it themselves. In a first meeting, I'm looking for the pain point — usually some form of revenue leakage, like a rate mismatch across OTA channels or a booking engine that isn't converting — and using that diagnosis to show them exactly where they're losing money and why it's worth fixing. That's the actual skill: not pushing a product, proving there's a real problem first. I'm building experience in the hotel industry day by day, while also staying close to new technology and AI tools so I keep pace with where the industry is heading.

Looking Ahead

Five years from now, I want to be someone known for that combination — real soft skills, real communication, and real technical ability together, not just one of the three.
